Marketing begins with a need

Marketing connects an understanding of customers with an offer that creates value for them and a viable return for the organisation.

The essential ideas

Identify & anticipate

Identify what customers need now; anticipate how needs and circumstances may change. Research comes before the offer.

Need, want, demand

A need is an underlying requirement. A want is a preferred way to satisfy it. Demand combines desire with willingness and ability to buy.

Satisfaction

Compare perceived performance with expectations: below expectations creates dissatisfaction; meeting them creates satisfaction; exceeding them may create delight.

Added example · apply the idea

A commuter needs mobility, wants a comfortable car, and creates demand when willing and able to pay. Installments change payment timing and affordability; they do not make the total cost disappear.

Clarification · read carefully

A customer may be the purchaser without being the end user. Other stakeholders matter, but “customer” and “stakeholder” are not interchangeable terms.

Need = the problem. Want = the preferred solution. Demand = a supported buying intention.

Check your understanding · added practice

A parent buys shampoo for a child. Who is the customer and who is the user?

Reveal the answer

The parent is the purchaser/customer; the child is the user. Research should consider the needs and influence of both.

Answer. The parent is the purchaser/customer; the child is the user. Research should consider the needs and influence of both.

Motivation & business orientation

An offer can satisfy practical and psychological needs at the same time. The business philosophy determines which needs receive attention.

The essential ideas

Mutual value

Customers receive benefits; the organisation needs sufficient revenue, funding or support to continue delivering them. Short-term profit alone is not a complete marketing purpose.

Production orientation

Prioritises availability, scale and efficient output. It can work when affordability and supply dominate demand.

Product & selling orientations

Product orientation emphasises features and quality; selling orientation emphasises persuading customers to buy existing output. Both can miss the customer’s actual problem.

Added example · apply the idea

A professional course might offer competence, career security, peer relationships and a sense of achievement. Several motivations can operate together.

Clarification · read carefully

Education is not itself a physiological need. Maslow’s categories are a discussion aid, not a rigid sequence that every customer follows. A brand cannot be assigned to one universal motivation.

Ask both “What does this product do?” and “What does owning it mean?”

Check your understanding · added practice

Why could excellent engineering still fail to produce a successful product?

Reveal the answer

The features may solve an unimportant problem, be too expensive, or be poorly matched to the chosen customer and buying context.

Answer. The features may solve an unimportant problem, be too expensive, or be poorly matched to the chosen customer and buying context.

A market-oriented organisation

Market orientation means the whole organisation uses customer and competitor insight to deliver superior value consistently.

The essential ideas

Customer & competitor understanding

Study changing customer needs and alternatives available to them. Compare competitors’ offers, capabilities and likely responses.

Coordination across functions

Marketing, operations, sales, finance and service need shared priorities. A promise is only useful when the organisation can fulfil it.

Culture & long-term value

The notes add a customer-focused culture and long-term shareholder value. Translate these into incentives and decisions that support lasting relationships.

Added example · apply the idea

A skincare brand promises reliable delivery. Marketing sets the expectation, stock planning supports availability, and service resolves failures. The customer experiences all three.

Clarification · read carefully

A USP is a distinctive selling claim; a value proposition explains the customer’s overall reason to choose the offer. Competitive advantage is the underlying ability to deliver better value or lower cost. The handwritten “Kotler said / CIM said” attribution is not verified.

Marketing sets the direction; selling is one activity within it.

Check your understanding · added practice

Why is a unique advertising slogan insufficient evidence of competitive advantage?

Reveal the answer

A slogan is a claim. Advantage requires an offer or capability customers value and rivals cannot easily match.

Answer. A slogan is a claim. Advantage requires an offer or capability customers value and rivals cannot easily match.

Start the plan with context

Before choosing a model, define the organisation, the decision, and the customer group your plan will address.

The essential ideas

Organisation background

State the name, purpose, main activities, geographic scope, relevant size and product/service range. Include only facts that influence the plan.

Stakeholders

Identify internal and external groups whose needs, decisions or resources affect delivery. Later, decide how each should be involved.

Customer scope

Choose a priority segment and distinguish buyer, user and influencer. Explain which product, market and time period the plan covers.

Added example · apply the idea

“This plan addresses repeat purchases of an existing shampoo range among time-poor parents in Cairo over the next 12 months.” This is a focused scope, not a claim about the actual business in your notes.

Clarification · read carefully

The 7Ps can be applied to goods as well as services. Culture can evolve, but a campaign usually has more direct influence on a specific behaviour than on deep cultural values. The notes’ timing claims about culture change are not general rules.

Context first; model second; recommendation last.

Check your understanding · added practice

Which is more useful in the introduction: every company milestone or evidence relevant to the planning problem?

Reveal the answer

Relevant evidence. Include a milestone only when it explains current capability, positioning, resources or constraints.

Answer. Relevant evidence. Include a milestone only when it explains current capability, positioning, resources or constraints.

Describe the business & its portfolio

A clear organisational profile explains what the business is equipped to do and how its products fit together.

The essential ideas

Type & scope

Distinguish commercial and nonprofit aims, domestic and international operations, and the stated legal form. These influence objectives and resources.

Size & trend

Use dated evidence on employees, revenue, units sold and capacity. Revenue growth can reflect price rises, so compare volume where relevant.

Portfolio dimensions

Width = number of product lines; length = total items across the mix; depth = variants of an item; consistency = how closely the lines relate.

Added example · apply the idea

Two product lines contain six product items in total. One shampoo comes in three sizes: width = 2, mix length = 6, and that item’s size depth = 3. Specify your counting convention.

Clarification · read carefully

The handwritten size thresholds lack a jurisdiction and dated source. Do not treat them as universal. The notes use “length” loosely for categories; the standard distinction is total items versus variants.

Width = lines; length = items; depth = variants; consistency = relationship.

Check your understanding · added practice

Adding a new fragrance to an existing shampoo increases which dimension most directly?

Reveal the answer

Depth, because it adds a variant of an existing product item. It does not necessarily create a new product line.

Answer. Depth, because it adds a variant of an existing product item. It does not necessarily create a new product line.

Portfolio choices: BCG & product life cycle

Use BCG to compare portfolio positions and the product life cycle to discuss how sales and profit may develop over time.

The essential ideas

BCG axes

Plot market growth against relative market share. Stars combine high growth and high share; cash cows combine low growth and high share. Question marks have high growth but low share; dogs/pets have both low.

Relative market share

Divide your share by the largest competitor’s share in the same defined market. A 25% share against a rival’s 50% gives 0.5×.

Product life cycle

Introduction, growth, maturity and decline describe a possible sales pattern. Profit and break-even timing depend on costs, price and investment.

Added example · apply the idea

A low-share product in a growing category is a question mark even if it has existed for years. Age alone does not locate a product in BCG.

Clarification · read carefully

BCG quadrants are not life-cycle stages. A cash cow is low growth, not high growth. Neither model proves profitability or prescribes an automatic decision.

BCG asks “Where are we positioned?” PLC asks “How is the product evolving?”

Check your understanding · added practice

Your share is 30% and your largest competitor has 20%. What is relative market share?

Reveal the answer

30 ÷ 20 = 1.5×. Use the competitor’s share as the denominator even when your business is the leader.

Answer. 30 ÷ 20 = 1.5×. Use the competitor’s share as the denominator even when your business is the leader.

Map customers, competitors & stakeholders

A marketing plan needs a picture of the relationships that make the organisation’s promise possible.

The essential ideas

Customer base

B2C serves consumers, B2B serves organisations, and B2G serves government buyers. Record the relevant buying process and value sought.

Competitive landscape

Identify major current rivals and compare their offers, market shares and dated performance evidence. Leader, challenger, follower and niche roles can aid description.

Stakeholder map

Your notes list internal employees, referrals, influence groups, suppliers, shareholders/owners, recruitment markets and customers. Customer markets continue on source page 8.

Added example · apply the idea

A distributor may be both a customer and a delivery partner. A pharmacist can influence choice without being the end user. Map each role to its impact on the plan.

Clarification · read carefully

Treat the “seven-market model” here as your lecturer’s grouping; relationship-marketing frameworks use different numbers and labels. Referrals can also come from existing customers. Do not infer collusion from supplier bargaining power.

For every stakeholder: interest, influence, evidence, response.

Check your understanding · added practice

Why should recruitment appear in a marketing plan for a service business?

Reveal the answer

The business may need scarce skills to deliver its service promise. Recruitment and employee retention therefore influence customer experience and growth capacity.

Answer. The business may need scarce skills to deliver its service promise. Recruitment and employee retention therefore influence customer experience and growth capacity.

SOSTAC: the spine of the plan

A planning framework connects evidence, choices, delivery and measurement. It prevents a collection of tactics from masquerading as a strategy.

The essential ideas

Start with the customer

Specify the segment, its needs and its current experience. Use research to establish a baseline before setting objectives.

Three planning lenses

MOST: mission, objectives, strategy, tactics. APIC: analysis, planning, implementation, control. SOSTAC adds a particularly useful structure for the full marketing plan.

Audit before SWOT

External analysis identifies opportunities/threats. Internal analysis establishes strengths/weaknesses. SWOT summarises the evidence; TOWS turns combinations into options.

Added example · apply the idea

Situation: repeat buying is weak. Objective: improve retention. Strategy: focus on a valuable customer group. Tactics: improve the offer and reminders. Action: assign owners. Control: track repeat rate and contribution.

Clarification · read carefully

Strategy states the route and choices. Tactics specify the mix. Action identifies who does what and when. Control measures progress and triggers changes. SWOT can be useful; an unsupported SWOT list is the problem.

Where are we? Where to? How? With what? Who and when? How are we doing?

Check your understanding · added practice

“Run three social posts weekly” belongs to which planning level?

Reveal the answer

It is a tactic/activity. The action plan adds ownership and timing; the objective specifies the outcome those posts are meant to support.

Answer. It is a tactic/activity. The action plan adds ownership and timing; the objective specifies the outcome those posts are meant to support.

PESTEL: political & economic forces

The macro environment contains broad forces that affect demand, costs, access and uncertainty. Select the factors that change a real business decision.

The essential ideas

Political factors

Consider government priorities, taxation policy, trade relationships, pressure groups, conflict and stability. Explain the specific route to business impact.

Economic factors

Assess interest rates, inflation, unemployment, incomes, GDP trends and the business cycle. The relevant indicator depends on the market.

Macro versus micro

PESTEL concerns wider conditions. Customers, suppliers and competitors sit closer to the business in the micro environment. Influence is a matter of degree, not a strict yes/no boundary.

Added example · apply the idea

Illustrative chain: higher financing costs → more expensive installments → weaker affordability for a durable good → test a lower-cost product or payment proposition.

Clarification · read carefully

The tax percentages and economic examples in the scan are classroom notes, not current economic facts. Aggregate GDP can be relevant, but sector and customer data often explain the decision better.

A PESTEL bullet is unfinished until you explain “so what for this organisation?”

Check your understanding · added practice

Is inflation always a threat to every business?

Reveal the answer

No. Its impact varies with input costs, pricing power, customer incomes and product category. State the mechanism and affected segment before classifying it.

Answer. No. Its impact varies with input costs, pricing power, customer incomes and product category. State the mechanism and affected segment before classifying it.

Economic exposure & social change

Good analysis connects economic conditions with how a specific group lives, spends and buys.

The essential ideas

Energy & income

Energy availability and cost influence production and distribution. Household income, disposable income and purchasing power influence affordability.

Demographics & distribution

Study age structure, household composition and income distribution. An average can hide major differences between segments.

Lifestyle

Health concerns, online shopping and time pressure may change what customers value. Check whether a trend applies to your segment and geography.

Added example · apply the idea

Time-poor customers might value scheduled delivery and clear product guidance more than a larger range. Test this through interviews and purchase data before investing.

Clarification · read carefully

The notes mention CAPMAS, central-bank data and international datasets as possible sources. Select a current dataset for the actual question; a correct statistic from another country may still be irrelevant.

Relevant, dated, local, decision-useful.

Check your understanding · added practice

Why can rising average income coexist with poor demand for your product?

Reveal the answer

Income gains may be concentrated outside the target segment, inflation may reduce real purchasing power, or the category may have weak priority in household spending.

Answer. Income gains may be concentrated outside the target segment, inflation may reduce real purchasing power, or the category may have weak priority in household spending.

Technology, environment & buying journeys

Changes in channels and technology alter both customer expectations and the capabilities needed to serve them.

The essential ideas

Webrooming & showrooming

Webrooming: research online, then buy in a store. Showrooming: inspect in a store, then buy online. Both suggest a connected journey across channels.

Technological scanning

Assess innovation, diffusion, obsolescence, communications and relevant production technology. Separate exciting claims from demonstrated usefulness.

Environmental factors

Consider resource use, waste, energy, climate exposure and sustainability expectations. Assess both the cost and opportunity for your business.

Added example · apply the idea

A retailer can support webrooming with accurate stock visibility and easy reservations. It can respond to showrooming through knowledgeable service, useful bundles and convenient fulfilment.

Clarification · read carefully

Gartner’s Hype Cycle describes technology expectations and maturity. It is not a product-sales forecast. Its sequence runs from an innovation trigger through inflated expectations and disillusionment to enlightenment and productivity.

Follow the customer’s journey, not the organisation’s channel chart.

Check your understanding · added practice

A customer tests headphones in a shop and orders them later on a website. Which behaviour is this?

Reveal the answer

Showrooming. The store supplied the physical evaluation while the transaction occurred online.

Answer. Showrooming. The store supplied the physical evaluation while the transaction occurred online.

Make the environmental audit actionable

Record the impact, timing and response for each relevant factor, then move from macro conditions to industry competition.

The essential ideas

Legal topics to investigate

Consumer protection, intellectual property, contracts, employment, competition, advertising and industry regulation may affect the plan. Identify the relevant jurisdiction and obtain current evidence.

A practical audit row

Record the factor, evidence/date, expected impact, time horizon, opportunity/threat, implication and proposed response. A single factor can create both upside and downside.

Industry analysis

Porter’s Five Forces examines rivalry, entrants, substitutes, supplier power and buyer power. Define the industry boundary before judging its attractiveness.

Added example · apply the idea

“A packaging requirement is expected next year; redesign may increase costs but could improve trust.” Specify the evidence and uncertainty before calling it a threat or opportunity.

Clarification · read carefully

The scan’s short/medium/long time bands are a classroom convention. Choose explicit bands that suit the planning horizon. Ethics can extend beyond legal compliance.

Evidence → impact → timing → implication → action.

Check your understanding · added practice

Why is a list of laws not yet a legal-environment analysis?

Reveal the answer

It does not explain applicability, business exposure, timing or the marketing decision that should change.

Answer. It does not explain applicability, business exposure, timing or the marketing decision that should change.

Rivalry & barriers to entry

Competition is a pattern of pressures within an industry, not simply a list of company names.

The essential ideas

Rivalry

Assess the balance of rivals, growth, product differentiation, fixed costs and exit barriers. Price cuts, service improvements and advertising can all be forms of rivalry.

Entry barriers

Scale, capital requirements, customer switching costs, distribution access, regulation and proprietary assets can make entry more difficult.

State a reasoned judgement

For each force, give a rating, supporting evidence and implication. Make clear whose perspective you are using: incumbent, entrant, buyer or supplier.

Added example · apply the idea

A specialised manufacturing process may require large investment and years of certification. A digital reseller of existing products may face much lower setup barriers.

Clarification · read carefully

High entry barriers and high exit barriers work differently. High entry barriers can deter new rivals; high exit barriers can keep struggling rivals in the market. Brand loyalty and differentiation do not automatically increase price rivalry.

Entry = getting in. Exit = getting out.

Check your understanding · added practice

Why can high exit barriers intensify rivalry?

Reveal the answer

Firms may keep competing despite weak returns because closure is expensive, assets are specialised, or obligations make leaving difficult.

Answer. Firms may keep competing despite weak returns because closure is expensive, assets are specialised, or obligations make leaving difficult.

Switching costs, substitutes & suppliers

The attractiveness of alternatives and the cost of changing them affect how much power each participant has.

The essential ideas

Switching costs

These are financial, time, learning, compatibility or relationship costs of changing provider. A price difference alone is not the full switching cost.

Substitute pressure

A substitute satisfies the same underlying need in a different way. Its price-performance appeal and ease of adoption influence the threat.

Supplier power

Few alternatives, essential inputs and difficult switching can strengthen suppliers. More suitable alternatives can weaken their bargaining position.

Added example · apply the idea

Moving from one software system to another may require data migration, staff retraining and process changes, even when the new subscription is cheaper. Those extra burdens are switching costs.

Clarification · read carefully

A competing shampoo brand is usually a rival in the same category. A fundamentally different way to achieve the desired outcome may be a substitute. Define the customer need and industry boundary explicitly.

Ask “What else solves the problem?” and “What would changing cost?”

Check your understanding · added practice

Would adding three qualified alternative suppliers usually raise or lower supplier power?

Reveal the answer

It would usually lower the incumbent supplier’s power, provided the alternatives have sufficient capacity and comparable quality.

Answer. It would usually lower the incumbent supplier’s power, provided the alternatives have sufficient capacity and comparable quality.

Buyer power, integration & complements

Bargaining power depends on alternatives and dependence. Integration changes who performs activities along the supply chain.

The essential ideas

Buyer power

Large purchases, strong information, low differentiation and easy switching can strengthen buyers. Loyalty and a valued distinctive offer can reduce their ability to force concessions.

Vertical integration

Backward integration moves into supply activities; forward integration moves toward distribution or the end customer. The implications depend on which firm integrates.

Complementers

Complements make an offer more valuable when used together, such as a device and compatible applications. Partnerships can improve adoption and convenience.

Added example · apply the idea

A producer acquiring a supplier integrates backward; opening its own retail channel is forward integration. The move may reduce dependence, but it also requires new capabilities and investment.

Clarification · read carefully

Complementers are an additional analytical lens, not a sixth force in Porter’s original framework. Their presence can create opportunities as well as dependency. The scan’s price-comparison websites are examples, not verified current recommendations.

Backward = toward inputs. Forward = toward customers.

Check your understanding · added practice

Why might a large retailer negotiate better terms than a small independent shop?

Reveal the answer

It may control greater order volume and access to customers, and may have more credible alternative suppliers.

Answer. It may control greater order volume and access to customers, and may have more credible alternative suppliers.

The marketing manager is accountable; CRM and operations specialists own defined tasks. Improve stock reliability before increasing demand through reminders.

4 · Calculations

Unit contribution = 100 − 60 = EGP 40.
Break-even incremental units = 20,000 ÷ 40 = 500.
Incremental contribution = 700 × 40 = EGP 28,000.
Campaign ROI = (28,000 − 20,000) ÷ 20,000 × 100 = 40%.
NPS = 50% − 20% = +30.
Delivery CSAT = 76 ÷ 100 × 100 = 76%.

Interpretation matters

The ROI depends on sales being incremental, full-price and measured after returns. The 700-unit forecast needs validation. NPS and delivery CSAT describe different questions; neither proves why repeat purchasing is weak. Track fulfilment, cohort repeat rate, contribution and qualitative feedback together.

Use the practice modes deliberately

  • Diagnostic: a 20-question starting sample with answers held until the end. It cannot assess every concept.
  • Topic practice: up to 10 questions, with immediate explanations.
  • Timed mixed practice: 20 questions using a time limit you choose. It is a pacing exercise, not an official-format mock.
  • Mistake review: revisit recorded wrong answers. A correct retry resolves the current flag while retaining attempt history.
